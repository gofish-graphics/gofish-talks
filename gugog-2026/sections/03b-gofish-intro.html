GoFish adds the verbs

a compositional language for visual structure · open source · gofish.graphics

chart level · big: short specs

chart(seafood, { axes: true })
  .flow(
    spread({
      by: "lake", dir: "x",
      spacing: 64
    }),
    stack({
      by: field("species")
        .sort("count"),
      dir: "y"
    })
  )
  .mark(rect({
    h: "count", fill: "species"
  }))
  .layer(ribbon({
    by: "species", opacity: 0.8
  }));

core · small: marks and relations

layer([rect(...).name("A"), circle(...).name("B")])
  .relate(({ A, B }) => [
    Constraint.distribute({dir: "x", spacing: 20}, [A, B]),
    ribbon({dir: "x"}, [A, B]),
  ])

Say: GoFish is my attempt at those verbs. It's a compositional language for visual structure, open source, in TypeScript and Python. Here's a ribbon chart. rect is the noun, the mark the grammar of graphics already has. The highlighted words are the verbs: spread lays the lakes out side by side, stack piles the species inside each lake, and ribbon is a relation that connects marks that are already placed. Verbs take graphics and compose them into bigger graphics.

Two levels (plant big vs. small here, one sentence): "GoFish has two levels. The chart level is a big language: short specs for common charts. Underneath is a small core, marks and relations like distribute and ribbon, and the chart level is built out of it. So GoFish already has both a big language and a small one, layered. I'll come back to that tradeoff at the end." Section 5 ("the open one: big vs. small") calls back to this slide.

The core listing is the paper's section 2.1 example (a distribute constraint and a ribbon relational mark in a layer + .relate()), copied exactly, (...) included. It is not the literal elaboration of the ribbon chart beside it; it shows the same verbs at the core level. The paper's wording: composite relations like spread "can be called outside of a .relate() method ... These direct calls allow users to construct hierarchical specifications without the verbose, but more expressive, layer + .relate() syntax."

The next cliff: a layout language more expressive than a fixed set of operators.

Hidden since 2026-11-06. Not counted in the running totals.

Target: 0:25 if shown.

"We replaced a typology of charts with a grammar. But our verbs are themselves a typology of operators, and that is the next cliff. Spread, stack, and relate are a fixed set. When you need a layout that is not in the set, you hit the cliff. Today you add it from outside the language, which is the same add-a-new-primitive move the grammar of graphics makes past its cliff. So we have to compress again."

"I think the way past it is a layout or constraint language that is more expressive than what we have now. That is the question I would most like help with."

Merged from the old "where we are now" and "evidence so far" slides. Points from those slides that are left out, for Q&A: traits keep the types smaller than they look, and the type system also runs out at extensible traits and at how distribution and temporal types interact with operators.

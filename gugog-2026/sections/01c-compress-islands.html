The lead into this slide is the question on the circle slide: "Why can't these grammars be unified with the grammar of graphics? Let's look at three of them."

This slide and the next two (PiCCL and GoTree) show the evidence before the next slide names what they added. The ideas are Atom's, PiCCL's, and GoTree's, and the credit goes to their authors.

Say: Here's Atom, by Park, Drucker, Fernandez, and Elmqvist. It is a grammar for unit visualizations, where one mark stands for one thing, like one passenger on the Titanic. Its marks are plain circles and rectangles. What Atom added is a small set of layout operators, like FillX and MaxFill. They split a space into cells and arrange the units inside them, and you nest them to place every unit.

Source: Park, Drucker, Fernandez and Elmqvist, "ATOM: A Grammar for Unit Visualizations", IEEE TVCG 2018. The top figure is the paper's teaser and the bottom one is its layout operator figure. Target: 0:10. Running total: 1:55 of 7:00.

Say: And here's GoTree, by Guozheng Li, Min Tian, Qinmei Xu, Michael McGuffin, and Xiaoru Yuan, at CHI 2020. It is a grammar for tree visualizations, like all of these. Its marks are plain nodes and links. What GoTree added is a small set of layout operators. On each axis, a parent and its children can be placed by include, juxtapose, or within, and siblings can be aligned or flattened. Mix those per axis and you get all of these layouts.

Lead into the next slide: "So what did these three grammars add? None of them mainly added a new kind of mark or channel."

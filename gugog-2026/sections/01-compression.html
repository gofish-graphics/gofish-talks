The Grammar of Graphics Needs Verbs Towards a GUGOG

Josh Pollock

7-minute talk at the GUGOG workshop, IEEE VIS 2026, Nov 9. It sets up the panel. The deck runs 7:50 at its targets against a 7:00 budget; the panel slide lists what to cut first.

The story: a visualization language is a compression of the charts people want to make. The grammar of graphics sits in the middle as one flat layer of fixed nouns. Programming languages do better by letting people define new abstractions from old ones, so the vocabulary grows as a tree, and verbs are what make that tree possible. GoFish adds verbs and has several API surfaces that share one core. A unified grammar faces three tensions. The first two look incidental, and the third, growing layouts from within the language, is the open question for the panel.

1 · Compression is the yardstick

the usage curve

Target: 0:20. Running total: 0:50 of 7:50.

  • The usage curve: how often a chart is made, against how complex it is. It is a long tail. Bar, line, and scatter are on the left. One-lab infographics and diagrams are on the right.
  • Name the two ends. Chart typologies like seaborn are on one end: a short call for each common chart, and a new function for every unusual one. Low-level toolkits like d3 are on the other end: a small vocabulary, and a long spec for every chart.
  • The curve is schematic, not measured.

1 · Compression is the yardstick

a good grammar is a good compression

Bar length: the size of the language, plus every spec written in it, weighted by how often each chart is made.

Target: 0:25. Running total: 1:15 of 7:50.

  • One function per chart type gives a big language. Specs stay short, but the language grows with every chart in the tail.
  • Low-level primitives give a tiny language and long specs, even for the common charts.
  • A good grammar keeps both small. The total cost is the size of the language plus the size of every spec written in it, weighted by how often each chart is made. That is the yardstick for the rest of the talk.
  • The bar sizes are schematic, not measured.

2 · The middle should be a tree

the middle is a tree, not a layer

Verbs build each level from the one below.

Target: 0:15. Running total: 2:00 of 7:50.

  • In programming languages and UI frameworks, people define new abstractions in terms of old ones, like components built from components. The vocabulary grows as a tree, and each level stays small.
  • Verbs, meaning composition operators like spread, stack, and relate, are what let a grammar do this.
  • Visualization languages are usually not built this way. The grammar of graphics is the flat layer on the left.

The picture: on the left, five charts are each written directly against one layer of fixed nouns. On the right, the same five charts sit on intermediate words (chart, tree, diagram) that are defined from a small core of marks and verbs. The leaves are charts from later in the talk: bar and ribbon from the crash course, node-link from GoTree, the waffle tree from the paper's Figure 6, and the planets diagram.

If asked about "optimal" compression: the honest claim is only that nested rules compress better than one flat layer. Grammar-based compression algorithms such as Sequitur work by building nested rules.

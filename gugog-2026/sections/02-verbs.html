If asked, or as backup:

  • Fair to the grammar of graphics: facets, coordinate transformations, and position adjustments like stack and dodge do combine marks. The paper's point is that they do not express all the constructions that the domain-specific grammars need.
  • The paper's own definitions (section 2): relations relate two or more marks. They can be spatial constraints, like alignment, or marks that connect other marks, like a ribbon. Operators are a way to write relations over arrays of data-driven marks, and an operator must lay out its children fully. So spread can be an operator, ribbon can be one because its children are already placed, and distribute cannot, because it only constrains one axis.
  • Relations come from the graphical perception literature: Gestalt grouping (common region, connection, alignment, proximity), Larkin and Simon on diagrams, and Pinker on how readers describe a graph.

Source: the GUGOG paper, section 2, "Background: GoFish Composition", and its introduction, which says that the grammar of graphics "lacks verbs that take graphical objects and compose them into new graphical objects".

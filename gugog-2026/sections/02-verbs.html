This is what the title of the paper means by verbs. Operators and relations are the verbs. Say "verbs" only if it helps; the slides use the two names.

If asked, or as backup (the hidden slide right after this one shows the precise version):

  • A relation can also draw. A ribbon or an arrow takes marks that are already placed and draws a new mark between them, without moving them. The paper calls these relational marks: "Like a typical mark, a relational mark can draw on the screen. However, unlike a typical mark, a relational mark takes children like other relations."
  • A relation does not hold its marks. It points to marks that something else holds, by name. GoFish calls such a name a ref. So the precise signature of align is align(ref, …, ref) → boolean, and of ribbon is ribbon(ref, …, ref) → mark.
  • Operators are made of relations. spread is a layer, which holds marks without placing them, plus align and distribute, which place every one. stack is the same spread with no gaps. Not every operator works this way. atop composites its pictures directly.
  • The paper's own definitions (section 2): relations relate two or more marks, either with spatial constraints, like alignment, or with marks that connect other marks, like a ribbon. Operators are a way to write relations over arrays of data-driven marks, and an operator must lay out its children fully. So spread can be an operator, ribbon can be one because its children are already placed, and distribute cannot, because it only constrains one axis.
  • Fair to the grammar of graphics: facets, coordinate transformations, and position adjustments like stack and dodge do combine marks. The paper's point is that they do not express all the constructions that the domain-specific grammars need.
  • Relations come from the graphical perception literature: Gestalt grouping (common region, connection, alignment, proximity), Larkin and Simon on diagrams, and Pinker on how readers describe a graph.

Source: the GUGOG paper, section 2, "Background: GoFish Composition", and its introduction, which says that the grammar of graphics "lacks verbs that take graphical objects and compose them into new graphical objects". The layer, align, and distribute definition of spread is in packages/gofish-graphics/src/ast/graphicalOperators/spread.tsx.

Operators hold marks, and relations point to them

spread(mark, …, mark) → mark

Operator. It holds its marks, places all of them, and makes one new mark around them.

align(ref, …, ref) → boolean

Relation. It points to marks held elsewhere. It is a fact, and GoFish places the marks so that it is true.

ribbon(ref, …, ref) → mark

Relational mark. It points to marks that are already placed and draws a new mark between them.

spread(a, b) = layer(a, b) where align(a, b) and distribute(a, b)

Most operators are a layer plus relations. A layer holds marks without placing them, and the relations place every one.

A ref is a name for a mark that something else holds.

Say: The same stack verb works on anything, which is the expressive part. The structure comes from the data's type: a unit declares what it can do, and stack needs values that add up. USD declared Trait.summable() stacks. Degrees Celsius doesn't have CanAdd, so the same chart is a loud error that names the missing capability and says what to do instead, not a wrong picture. "This is one case where the tradeoff looks incidental. CanAdd constrains specs, not pictures: every arrangement is still reachable. To stack °C you either declare the unit summable, which is a deliberate claim about your data, or you stack the means, which is the fix the error itself suggests. The only thing it takes away is stacking numbers whose sums mean nothing."

Fair framing:

  • This is the idea Cynthia and Matt call Error Friction (their section 4 design principles and section 6.3): invalid specifications are made costly to reach, while valid ones stay fluid.
  • It blocks the hazard they name in section 2 ("Motivating Contributions"): if linked selection were added without validity checks, "the grammar would silently permit combinations, such as stacking of group means". A .mean() result is not summable, so stack refuses it.
  • Limits, said plainly: this is one operator and one property. Bartonicek et al.'s actual setting, linked highlighting in interactive graphics, is not addressed here. "I haven't found a principled tradeoff yet" is not "there isn't one."
  • Their paper calls the two kinds of contribution "distinct and complementary" (section 5.1). Present CanAdd in that spirit: a mechanism for them to push on, not a rebuttal.

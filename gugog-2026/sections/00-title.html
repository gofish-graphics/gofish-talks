The story: the talk opens on a wall of GoFish charts. The grammar of graphics wrote the common charts in one shared language. For the long tail of charts, we built many new grammars instead, and they are hard to compare because each one is its own language. I suspect, but am not sure, that this makes new research questions harder to ask. I think a shared core exists, but it may not look quite like the grammar of graphics. A visualization language is a compression of the charts people want to make, and the question for the talk is what stands in the way of doing that compression well. The grammar of graphics has a rich set of nouns but almost no verbs. GoFish adds verbs, and with them it can write other grammars' ideas in one language. Part 4 takes on the central tradeoff from the compression chart: a big language that covers many charts, or a small one with long specs. Steele's answer is a language that can grow, and with verbs it grows as a tree, with a small core and new levels built from old ones. Extensibility keeps the levels one language. The objection is that this much growth costs safety, and a small type system brings safety back. GoFish can grow charts this way but not yet layouts, and that is the open question for the panel.

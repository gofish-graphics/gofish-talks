The Grammar of Graphics Needs Verbs Towards a GUGOG

Josh Pollock

7-minute talk at the GUGOG workshop, IEEE VIS 2026, Nov 9. It sets up the panel. The deck runs 7:00 at its targets; the panel slide lists what to cut first if it runs long.

The story: the talk opens on a wall of GoFish charts. Then the hook, over the islands drawing: I love visualization grammars, and I'm sad, because I can't use them together. They are different systems that don't work with each other, and a lot of good work has no clear path into practice. Why are there so many islands? The compression chart answers it. The grammar of graphics compresses the common charts very well, then hits the expressivity cliff, and each island is someone getting past the cliff on their own. So what if we compress the islands? To make a description shorter, you have to find the structure (Simon, 1962), and a language with only nouns finds nouns. Atom and PiCCL each added operators, so the grammar of graphics needs verbs. GoFish adds them, and Atom and PiCCL reduce to them. Two fair objections follow. Andrew McNutt's is that one language cannot serve every domain's design; with verbs, a DSL is a library built inside the language, as GoTree shows. Cynthia Huang and Matthew Kay's is that growth costs safety; a small type system brings it back, as CanAdd shows. Our verbs are themselves a typology of operators, and that is the next cliff and the first question for the panel.

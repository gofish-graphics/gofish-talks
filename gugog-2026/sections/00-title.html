The Grammar of Graphics Needs Verbs Towards a GUGOG

Josh Pollock

7-minute talk at the GUGOG workshop, IEEE VIS 2026, Nov 9. It sets up the panel. The deck runs 7:00 at its targets; the panel slide lists what to cut first if it runs long.

The story: the talk opens on a wall of GoFish charts. Why unify at all? The old reasons, easier to write and easier to read, matter less now that AI writes a lot of code. The reason that still holds is that compressing forces you to find structure. The grammar of graphics compressed many chart types into one shared language, and that revealed marks, channels, scales, and coordinate systems. The compression chart shows how well today's designs compress, up to the expressivity cliff. Past the cliff, we built islands: specialized grammars that cannot reach each other. What the islands share is operators, so the grammar of graphics needs verbs. GoFish adds them, and Atom and PiCCL reduce to them. Two fair objections follow. Andrew McNutt's is that one language cannot serve every domain's design; with verbs, a DSL is a library built inside the language, as GoTree shows. Cynthia Huang and Matthew Kay's is that growth costs safety; a small type system brings it back, as CanAdd shows. Our verbs are themselves a typology of operators, and that is the next cliff and the first question for the panel.

chart(titanicPassengers)
  .flow(
    spread({ by: "pclass", dir: "x",
      spacing: 24, alignment: "end" }),
    derive(rows => orderBy(rows,
      ["survived"], ["desc"])),

The PiCCL bottle chart in GoFish

chart(bottleData, { axes: false })
  .flow(spread({ by: "category", dir: "x", ... }))
  .mark(layer([
    atop({ blendMode: "color" }, [
      image({ href: bottlePng, h: v(100), ... }),
      rect({ h: "amount", ... }),
    ]).name("bottle"),
    rect({ h: 1, y: "amount", ... }).name("line"),
    text({ text: (d) => `${d.amount}%`, ... }).name("label"),
  ]).relate(({ bottle, line, label }) => [
    Constraint.align({ x: "start" }, [bottle, line]),
    Constraint.distribute({ dir: "y", ... }, [line, label]),
    Constraint.align({ x: "end" }, [label, line]),
  ]))

The callout (since 2026-11-05; reworded 2026-11-06, because the core slide it pointed back to is hidden): the blue tinted lines, tagged "core", are GoFish's lower level, where you write relations directly: layer, and .relate(...) with Constraint.align and Constraint.distribute. The rest of the spec is the chart API from the crash course. Say: "And look at the tinted lines. Here the relations are written directly: line up the line with the bottle, and put the label above the line. The operators and the relations mix in one spec, because they are one language."

Say: This bottle chart comes from PiCCL, by Shi and others at VIS 2025. It is built from the PiCCL parts we saw earlier. A picture of a bottle gets a green rectangle drawn on top of it, as tall as the number, and only the part inside the bottle shows. A line and a label mark the top of the green. In GoFish, those same parts are a few operators, relations, and marks. spread repeats the bottle for each category. atop draws the green rectangle onto the bottle picture and keeps only the part inside the bottle, like PiCCL's own atop. layer adds the line and the label, and relate lines them up with the top of the green. So PiCCL's idea fits into GoFish as a few operators and marks, and you do not need a new language for it.

The point of the two comparisons: these are the same pictures from earlier, now written with one shared set of operators and relations. The same spread does the work of Atom's gridxy and of PiCCL's repeating. Written in one language, the two islands share most of their structure, and the differences are small parts you can point to. What the two islands share is operators and relations.

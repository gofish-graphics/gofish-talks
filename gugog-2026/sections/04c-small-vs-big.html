4c · A small language against a big one

I should not design a small language, and I should not design a large one. I need to design a language that can grow.

Guy L. Steele Jr., Growing a Language, OOPSLA 1998

Target: 0:15. Running total: 6:30 of 7:50.

  • Tie it to part 1. A small core keeps the language small, and growing from within gives the big vocabulary that short specs need.
  • A small core and an extensible core are not in tension. An extensible core can be small, as long as people extend it from within the language. Growing from within is Steele's answer to the choice between small and big.

Source: Guy L. Steele Jr., "Growing a Language", keynote at OOPSLA 1998, published in Higher-Order and Symbolic Computation 12(3), 1999. where we are now

so far

  • Charts grow from within.
  • Marks reduce to simple primitives.
  • Interfaces reduce to bounding boxes and boundary curves.

not yet

  • Layouts are opaque plug-ins.
  • The verbs are a fixed set.

Target: 0:20. Running total: 6:50 of 7:50.

  • GoFish can grow from within at the chart level. GoTree and the chart templates are defined with the same verbs people use to build graphics.
  • Marks reduce to simple primitives, and interfaces reduce to bounding boxes and boundary curves. So the core can be small.
  • Layouts cannot grow from within yet. They are opaque plug-ins written outside the language. The v3 draft points out the same problem for ggplot2, where extending it with ggproto means leaving the plot language for a package implementation interface.
  • The verbs are still a fixed set.

Merged from the old "where we are now" and "evidence so far" slides. Points from those slides that are left out, for Q&A: traits keep the types smaller than they look, and the type system also runs out at extensible traits and at how distribution and temporal types interact with operators.

From thesis deck:

Data comes first, because everything after it is a mapping from it. This is the seafood catch table the whole rest of the talk runs on: one row per lake and species, with a count. Thirty rows, three fields. Point at it and say the audience will see this exact table again in a few minutes when GoFish builds its first chart -- it is the same data, and that is deliberate.

Marks are the shapes; channels are how data maps onto them, with Munzner's famous accuracy ranking: position and length beat angle, area, and color. Coordinate systems can transform the space those marks are drawn in, as Thompson's growth transformation shows. That's the grammar's vocabulary for precision: how exactly one value can be read. But marks and channels are blind to arrangement, to how things are grouped and laid out. The next slides pick up that thread: GoFish asks where the chart's spatial structure went.

Takeaway: Marks, channels, and coordinates say how values are read — none of them name the chart's spatial structure. GoFish asks where it went.
